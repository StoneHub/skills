# Claims, honesty, and brand safety

A promo that gets called out in the comments costs more than it earns. Building trust is part of the craft.

- **Numbers and claims:** use the company's published, approved wording verbatim (site, release notes, decks) and note the source. Don't invent stats, timings or speed claims. "Faster" needs a real measurement or first-person framing.
- **Dramatizations:** fictional calls, users or scenarios get a visible "Dramatization" label, and they shouldn't look like real customer data. Use fictional phone numbers (555) and generic names.
- **Mockups vs. evidence:** an animated recreation of the product is fine as illustration. Before it's presented as a demo, suggest swapping in or pairing it with a real recording.
- **Competitors:** frustration lands better and safer in first person ("I got tired of fighting X") than as a universal claim ("X never works"). Don't use competitors' logos or exact UI. A generic parody gets the point across.
- **Third-party marks:** platform and partner logos need permission or a clear nominative-use reason. Drop them from ads by default.
- **Personal data:** blur or replace names, phone numbers and notifications in screenshots and recordings unless they're already public and approved for marketing.
- **Feature status:** features that are beta, gated or "on request" should be framed accordingly, or flagged for marketing sign-off.
- **Credentials:** API keys stay in the user's environment. Never put them in chat, files or logs.
- **Publishing:** never post, upload or send the video anywhere without the user's explicit go-ahead.

At delivery, list what's dramatized, what's recreated rather than real, any font substitutions, and anything that needs sign-off.
